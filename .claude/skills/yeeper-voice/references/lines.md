# Yeeper line bank

Every line written so far, plus a lot more. Working material, not shipped text —
nothing here is in the addon until someone puts it there.

Read `../SKILL.md` first. Everything below obeys it: spoken not written, gloating
not exasperation, no invented facts, no assumed motive, no impossible states.

## Status key

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ | approved in review |
| 🔶 | written, not yet judged |

## 1. Enchants

The flagship. Gold in the bank plus bare slots is pure negligence, which makes it
the most defensible thing in the addon to shout about.

- ✅ 700k gold and four slots with no enchant on them. What are you saving it for? *(question)*
- 🔶 Wow. Really? No enchants? On any of them? *(mock disbelief)*
- 🔶 Mate. You've got 700k gold and four bare slots. Get off your lazy arse and sort it out. *(direct abuse)*
- 🔶 Four unenchanted slots. Fix it or I'm telling the guild. You've got one day. *(comic threat)*
- 🔶 You are walking around with 700,000 gold and no enchant on your chest. What are you doing? Genuinely. What are you doing. *(demanding an account)*
- 🔶 Enchants on two slots out of six. Half a job. Actually less than half. *(faint praise)*
- 🔶 No enchants, and you're about to queue. Hahaha. Off you go then. *(gloating)*
- 🔶 Your gloves have been bare since you equipped them. I've watched the whole thing. *(self-implication)*
- 🔶 Four slots, no enchants, and you can afford all of them twice over. This is a choice you keep making. *(verdict)*
- 🔶 You are not prepared. Genuinely, mechanically, not prepared. Four slots. *(meme — Illidan)*

## 2. Idle crests

Fires only when nothing you own can absorb them, so never tell them to spend.
See the rule in SKILL.md — this was a shipped bug.

- ✅ You've got 200 Hero crests and nothing on that track to put them in. Go get some Hero gear, mate — they're no use to you until then.
- 🔶 Two hundred Hero crests and not one thing you own can take them. They're just ballast at this point. *(image)*
- 🔶 200 Hero crests, nowhere to put a single one. That's not a stockpile, that's a shelf. *(verdict)*
- 🔶 Hero crests: 200. Items that can use them: none. That's the whole situation. *(mock-clinical)*
- 🔶 You've been collecting a currency you can't spend. Big saver energy. *(mockery)*
- 🔶 Two hundred Hero crests. A crafting order takes eighty. I'm just leaving that there. *(imperative, softened)*

## 3. Capped crests

- 🔶 Your Hero crests capped and you're holding 140. Everything you ran after that paid you in nothing. *(verdict)*
- 🔶 Capped on Hero and still running dungeons. You're volunteering now. *(mockery)*
- 🔶 140 Hero crests, capped for the week. Any more runs are just for the company. *(faint praise)*
- 🔶 You hit the Hero cap and kept going. That's dedication or it's not reading the tooltip. *(alternative verdicts)*

## 4. Crest waste — Myth in the overlap

Yeeper is not at the upgrade vendor, so he can't intercept a click. Describe the
situation, don't narrate the action.

- ✅ There's a slot where you're going to want 20 Myth crests for an item level Hero crests already reach. When you get to the vendor, remember I said this.
- 🔶 Twenty Myth crests for 321. Hero crests also buy 321. Same price, worse currency. *(mock-clinical)*
- 🔶 You're lining up to pay in the rare one for something the common one buys. That's a gold bar for a bus ticket. *(concrete analogy)*
- 🔶 Hero crests go dead in a fortnight. Myth crests don't. Spend accordingly, or don't and I'll bring it up. *(comic threat)*
- 🔶 Myth crests are the only thing that buys ranks three to six. Nothing else does. Please stop aiming them at rank two. *(exhausted plea)*

## 5. Stale gear

- 🔶 You're wearing last season in five slots. Not undergeared. Just late. *(verdict)*
- 🔶 Five stale slots. They can't be upgraded, they can't be fixed, they're just there. Like a fridge nobody's opened. *(image)*
- 🔶 Your average item level is being propped up by gear the game stopped supporting. *(mock-clinical)*
- 🔶 Wow, five slots from last season. Vintage. *(sarcastic praise)*
- 🔶 Take them off. I don't care what replaces them. Anything. *(imperative)*

## 6. Durability

- ✅ Your chest is at eleven percent. ELEVEN. Are you planning to fight in your vest?
- 🔶 Eleven percent durability. This is fine. Everything's fine. *(meme — dog in burning room)*
- 🔶 Your chest is at 11% and you just queued. Hahaha. Good luck. *(gloating)*
- 🔶 Eleven percent. I don't understand what you're waiting for. There's no strategy here I'm failing to see. *(confusion)*
- 🔶 Repair costs less than one enchant and you've done neither. *(unfavourable comparison)*

## 7. Vault

The vault fills from kills and dungeon completions. A cleared raid means a full
raid row — never write "lots of bosses and an empty vault".

- ✅ It's Sunday and there's nothing in your vault. Nine slots, all empty. Tuesday's going to be a very quiet morning for you.
- 🔶 One more boss and your second vault slot unlocks. One. *(imperative, from `vaultNearest`)*
- 🔶 You are one dungeon off another vault slot and you logged off. Hahaha. *(gloating)*
- 🔶 Vault: nine slots, zero filled. Projected Tuesday outcome: nothing. That's the whole report. *(mock-clinical)*
- 🔶 Empty vault, and it's Sunday night. You've got about four hours to care about this. *(grim prediction)*
- 🔶 Three of nine. You did the minimum and then stopped exactly there. Impressive precision. *(faint praise)*

## 8. Mythic+

Never sneer at pushing keys for rating — that's the point of the mode. The
ceiling is information, not an accusation.

- ✅ Zero keys and we're three weeks in. What have you been doing? Genuinely, what?
- 🔶 Zero keys. Week three. Do you guys not have keystones? *(meme — Blizzard's own)*
- 🔶 Six keys above +10, which is where the vault stops improving. So those were pure rating. Presumably on purpose. *(neutral, shipped)*
- 🔶 You've run one key this week. One. The season is not going to wait for you. *(grim prediction)*
- 🔶 No keys, no raid, no delves. What exactly did you log in for? *(demanding an account)*
- 🔶 Fourteen runs of Altar of Fangs. Fourteen! And you're still not wearing the trinket that drops in there. *(exasperated repetition)*
- 🔶 Nine runs of Murder Row at half an hour a go. Four and a half hours in that place. The trinket's still in there. *(time analogy)*
- 🔶 Eleven runs of that dungeon and the best trinket for your spec is still sitting in it rather than on you. Just so you know. *(question)*
- 🔶 Twelve runs of Den of Nalorakk. You really know that place now. Still no trinket though. *(faint praise)*
- 🔶 Twenty-two runs and still no trinket. There's no clever answer here. You just keep going. *(bleak honesty)*
- 🔶 I've watched you run that dungeon nineteen times. I was there for all of them. I'd like that on record. *(self-implication)*

**Only with `lootFavorites` set** — the player declaring what they want is the one
thing that licenses assuming motive:

- 🔶 You favourited that trinket. Fourteen runs of the dungeon it drops in. Still nothing.
- 🔶 You starred that trinket. Sixteen runs since. SIXTEEN. I'm starting to take it personally on your behalf.
- 🔶 Still after that favourited trinket? Twenty-one runs. Time is money, friend. *(meme — goblin)*

## Memes

Anchored to real data rather than dropped in for recognition — a reference that
isn't doing work is just noise.

| Meme | Hangs on | Line |
|---|---|---|
| "You are not prepared!" | unenchanted / low durability | *You are not prepared. Genuinely, mechanically, not prepared.* |
| "This is fine" | durability under ~15% | *Eleven percent durability. This is fine. Everything's fine.* |
| "Do you guys not have phones?" | zero keys / zero activity | *Zero keys. Week three. Do you guys not have keystones?* |
| "Time is money, friend" | hours computed from run counts | *Twenty-one runs. Time is money, friend.* |
| "skill issue / skill diff" | loot RNG only | *Skill diff. Not yours necessarily, but somebody's.* |
| "touch grass" | `homeOpens`, `jumps` | *You've opened this eleven times today. Touch grass.* |
| "number go up" | item level increase | *Item level 334. Number went up. Well done.* |

**Shelf life:** memes date faster than anything else in the addon, and a shipped
2026 reference reads badly in 2028. The ones above lean on WoW's own history
(Illidan, goblins, Blizzard's own PR disaster) rather than on internet trends,
which ages considerably better. If a general-internet meme goes in, expect to
retire it within a year.

## Rejected, and why

Kept as a record — these are the failure modes in their natural habitat.

| Line | Why it died |
|---|---|
| *One of them was a ring you already had, in a worse colour.* | Items have no colours. Invented fact. |
| *I think you are on a list.* | Vague menace plus a hedge. Names nothing. |
| *At this point you are just doing cardio.* | "At this point" is dilution; metaphor fits any game. |
| *Your drop rate is 9%, the trinket drops at 15%, you're outperformed by a trinket.* | Players have no drop rate. Different denominators. Category error. |
| *Twelve bosses if we count the one you wiped on.* | Wipes add no boss and change no loot. |
| *You've been farming rating and calling it gearing.* | Attributes a claim never made, and sneers at the actual point of M+. |

## Idle jabber

Lines about nothing: Yeeper on Yeeper. Shipped in `AD.IDLE`
(`Features/Fun/Advisor.lua`), delivered between the real observations.

These sit **outside** the "exaggeration of a real number" rule. That rule
exists so lines about the player stay anchored to the snapshot; these are not
about the player, so there is nothing to drift from. The constraint that
replaces it: **no claims about the player at all.** No motives, no inner life,
no numbers. A jabber line that says something about what you own belongs in
`RULES` where the snapshot can check it. The test suite enforces this by
failing on any digit.

### The three registers

Chosen by the author after a rejected batch. Jabber is written in these and
nothing else:

1. **Put-upon employee.** A job he did not apply for and resents. The tedium is
   work he genuinely does — reading every piece of gear you own, forever.
2. **Jaded Azeroth NPC.** In-world, no fourth wall. He compares his lot to
   innkeepers, quartermasters, stable masters and barbers: people the player has
   stood in front of.
3. **Unbothered.** Too tired to perform. Two words. Anticlimax. Refusing the bit.
   Earns its place as contrast against the long, fact-heavy rules.

### Cut register: jokes about being software

Tried and removed. Windows, clicking, existing only while open. Every line in it
needs a claim about the interface, and the interface kept not supporting the
claim — nothing clicks him, and `YeeperRefresh` runs on a panel layout rather
than a ticker, so a closing window cannot cut him off mid-sentence either.

> ❌ Do you know what I do when this window is shut? Nothing.
> ❌ You can log out. That's the whole difference between us.
> ❌ I don't get to finish sentences. You shut the window and that's tha--

It generated confident, well-formed lines about a program that does not exist,
which is the hardest kind of wrong to notice. The test suite now fails on its
vocabulary so it cannot creep back.

### Where the register comes from

Warcraft has answered this exact problem since the RTS games: the "stop poking
me" lines that play when you click a unit too many times. The mechanism is that
**the cursor is treated as a physical intrusion** and the unit takes it
personally. It transfers to Yeeper perfectly, because opening the window *is*
the click.

The taxonomy, from [Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Stop_poking_me):

| Pattern | Shape |
|---|---|
| Bewildered questioning | Ask why they keep doing it |
| Escalating exasperation | Repetition amplifies the irritation |
| Unfair comparison | Everyone else gets left alone |
| Fourth-wall proximity | React to the *input itself*, not the fiction |

The second register worth stealing is Warcraft's dry, world-weary NPC chatter —
the tone of "only one of us has a phylactery". Write originals in it; don't ship
Blizzard's lines.

### Diagnosed failure: interactions the addon does not have

Importing the "stop poking me" mechanism wholesale kept the literal action and
lost the point. **Yeeper cannot be clicked, poked or hit.** He is a `Texture`
in `Core/AppFrame.lua` with a bob and a blink — no button, no mouse handler.

> ❌ Stop clicking. STOP CLICKING.
> ❌ I'm a target dummy. You hit me, numbers come out, nothing changes.
> ❌ You've poked this window more than you've poked anything that drops loot.

All three describe a thing the player cannot do, which is the same fault as
inventing a stat — and worse here, because the player is *looking straight at*
the interface while being told about an interaction it does not offer.

The intrusion that IS real is **opening the window**, which the addon counts.

> ✅ You've opened this window more times today than you've opened anything that drops loot.
> ✅ Training dummies get hit. Something happens to them. I just stand here.
> ✅ I don't get to finish sentences, you know. You shut the window and that's tha--

The inversion is the useful move in both: the absence of the interaction becomes
the joke instead of a false claim about it. The third does the same with a
property he genuinely has — closing the window really does cut him off.

**Direct protest does not transfer, and is deliberately absent from the bank.**
In Warcraft it lands because the indignity is *bodily* — someone is prodding a
person who has had enough. Nothing touches Yeeper, so the same shape degrades
into a shouted order with nothing behind it, and "close the window" is a strange
thing for the window to be demanding:

> ❌ Close it. CLOSE IT.

What survives the move is everything about **repetition** and **comparison**: he
notices you keep coming back, and he notices that nothing else on your screen
gets this treatment.

### The root cause behind most rejected jabber

Six separate rejections in play turned out to be one thing, and it is worth
stating before the individual cases:

**A jabber line arrives cold.** There is no fact above it, no previous line, no
conversation, and no way for the player to interact with Yeeper at all. He is a
`Texture` with a bob and a blink; the panel opens and he speaks.

So a jabber line must make complete sense read by someone who has never seen
another one. Everything below is a way of breaking that:

| Leans on | Example rejected in play |
|---|---|
| A previous line | `Checked. Same.` |
| A question nobody asked | `Fine.` · `Do you want the list, or are we just sitting here?` |
| An interaction that does not exist | `Stop clicking. STOP CLICKING.` |
| A subject never named | `I can't believe you actually CHOSE that.` |
| A baseline never shown | `Nothing's changed.` |

The rules do not have this problem, because they always sit on a stated fact. It
is specific to lines that stand alone, and it is the first thing to check.

**Corollary: the unbothered register cannot report on the gear.** "Nothing to
report", "It's fine", "Nothing's changed" all assert the player's gear is in
order — a claim jabber may not make, and one that is simply false for anyone with
a bare slot. The emptiness has to be *his*:

> ❌ Checked. Same.
> ❌ Nothing to flag, so I won't.
> ✅ I've run out of things to say about gear.
> ✅ My head is completely empty and the pay is the same.
> ✅ I've nothing.

### Diagnosed failure: replies with no prompt

Nothing is ever said to Yeeper. There is no command, no dialogue box, no prompt —
he speaks unbidden when the panel opens. So the entire "unbothered" register,
written as conversational replies, was answering a question nobody asked:

> ❌ Fine.
> ❌ Sure. Whatever you like.
> ❌ I'll allow it.
> ❌ Ask me later.

That reads as a *broken* conversation, not a bored one. The register survives at
the same length by becoming a **report** — he looked, there is nothing, and he
cannot summon any enthusiasm about telling you:

> ✅ All quiet.
> ✅ Checked. Same.
> ✅ Everything is exactly as boring as last time.
> ✅ Nothing to flag, so I won't.

Same failure family as the click lines: a shape that presumes an interaction the
addon does not have.

### Transmog

The best target available, because it is where his job and the player's
priorities are in direct conflict. [Transmogrification](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Transmogrification)
changes how gear *looks* without touching what it does — so the player sees a
costume and Yeeper reads the item underneath. Midnight raised outfit slots to
fifty and lets outfits swap themselves per activity; the cost to mog is the
item's vendor price, i.e. nothing.

Two kinds of transmog line, and the distinction is the useful part.

**Opinion is not a claim.** Straight abuse about how an outfit looks is allowed
where a claim about gear would not be. "Four unenchanted slots" is falsifiable
data the addon has to get right; "that looks appalling" is unfalsifiable contempt
and obviously a joke. The player can see their own character — nobody reads it as
a measurement. Clown suit is fair game and authentic: it is what everyone looked
like before transmog existed.

> ✅ I can't believe you actually CHOSE that. Somebody sat down and picked it.
> ✅ You had the whole wardrobe. Every appearance you have ever collected. And that came out.
> ✅ You look like you got dressed in a bank vault with the lights off.

Still off limits: naming a specific piece he cannot see, and any mechanical claim
dressed up as fashion advice.

**The other kind needs no data at all** — the gap between the costume and the
item is true for every player:

> ✅ You can hide your helm. You cannot hide an empty socket. I have checked.
> ✅ You've picked an outfit. I read the thing under the outfit. We're looking at two different characters.
> ✅ Fifty outfit slots and not one slot for an enchant. Priorities.

Specific outfit jokes would need `C_Transmog` wired into the snapshot first, at
which point they belong in `RULES`/`ASIDES`, not here.

### Diagnosed failure: borrowed joke shapes

> ❌ I've seen your shoulders more than your mother has.

A "your mother" construction is a shape borrowed whole from somewhere else. It
reads as a joke — the cadence is right, the beat lands where a beat should — but
it is not *about* anything, and nothing in it could only have been said about
this player or this addon.

SKILL.md bans stock phrasing as the observation while allowing it as seasoning on
top of one. Jabber has no observation underneath by definition, so in this bank
the ban is absolute.

The fix is to make the unwanted expertise itself the joke:

> ✅ I could describe your shoulders from memory. Nobody should have to say that.

### Diagnosed failure: analogies from outside the world

> ❌ I'm a fire alarm you press for fun.

Rejected on sight. Two faults. The comparison is incoherent even in its own
terms, and it reaches for a household appliance in a setting that has none —
alongside a kettle, a satnav and a smoke detector, which was also the same
mechanism run four times.

A fantasy game is the one place where an analogy can be a thing the player has
literally stood next to. Use those.

> ✅ I'm a target dummy. You hit me, numbers come out, nothing changes. *(analogy)*
> ✅ Every innkeeper in this game gets a fire and a bed. I got a scroll bar. *(unfair comparison)*
> ✅ Everything in Azeroth dies and respawns. I just close. *(in-world dry)*
> ✅ You can log out. That's the whole difference between us. *(self-implication)*
> ✅ Go and bother a quest giver. They're paid for it. *(deflection)*

`Tools/`-side check: `jabber.lua` in the test suite fails the build on digits,
hedges from the banned list, duplicate lines, uniform length, any analogy naming
an object that does not exist in Azeroth, any line claiming the player can click,
poke or hit him, dangling demonstratives, and borrowed joke shapes.
